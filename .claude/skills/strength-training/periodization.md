# Periodization — matching gym work to the season

"If your in-season gym looks like your off-season gym, you're doing it wrong." The exercises change
less than the **dose and intent**. Read the current block from `docs/personal/season.md`; it sets
frequency and phase. This file maps Peden's off/in-season model onto this athlete's blocks.

## The two poles

|                    | **Off-season / build**                         | **In-season / race phase**                        |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Goal               | **Build** capacity; fix last season's limiters | **Maintain** what you built; protect riding       |
| Affordable fatigue | Higher — recovery time is on your side         | Low — recovery is the priority                    |
| Volume             | More sets, more variety                        | **Less** (the biggest lever)                      |
| Range of motion    | Full                                           | **Reduced** (box/pin/low-box)                     |
| Load / intent      | Heavier; can chase force                       | Slightly lighter, **moved fast**, auto-regulated  |
| Novelty            | New exercises OK (they cost fatigue)           | Stick to known movements                          |
| Reps               | Can run higher after a break (GPP)             | **Never above 12; low single-digits is the norm** |

## The in-season rule of three ("reduce")

When maintaining through a race phase, apply all three:

1. **Reduce range of motion.** Box squats, pin squats, low-box step-ups — lower soreness, still a
   meaningful neuromuscular stimulus. Save full range for winter.
2. **Reduce volume.** _The most important lever._ Few working sets; pick the key exercises, drive
   high quality to a low number of sets/reps, and get out. Reps **>6 only** after a break when you're
   re-doing general prep; otherwise stay heavy and low.
3. **Reduce load slightly and move it fast.** Don't chase bar numbers in-season. Auto-regulate on how
   you feel; **focus on acceleration**; stay away from failure. Grinding buys fatigue that kills the
   next ride.

**Reduce ROM · reduce volume · reduce load + move fast + highest quality every rep.** That's how you
maintain (sometimes even improve) while the bike stays the priority.

## Rep/intent guide (auto-regulated, RPE-led)

- **Max strength / force ceiling:** ~**3–6 reps**, RPE ~7–8, leaving reps in reserve; heavy but never
  grinding. Once competent — matches the philosophy's "low-rep heavy" pillar.
- **Power / RFD:** low reps (**~3–5**), sub-maximal load, **maximal bar/jump velocity**; stop the set
  when speed drops. Jumps ~**2–4 × 3–5**.
- **GPP / re-entry after a break only:** higher reps (8–12) briefly to re-groove before loading.
- Never prescribe kg — the athlete auto-regulates by RPE and by whether the bar/jump is still fast.

## Placement within the week (hard constraints)

From the `coaching-philosophy` execution rules — these override any template:

- **Stack strength on a hard ride day** (ride AM / gym PM, a few hours apart) so easy days stay easy.
- **No high-intensity bike day the day _after_ heavy lifting.**
- **Never a fatiguing gym day the day before a track session** — protect track quality.
- **Two-a-days only on weekends / WFH days.** With 2×/week, that usually means both gym sessions land
  on or beside the two weekend quality days.
- Recovery weeks (every 3–4 build weeks) cut gym volume too — drop a session or halve the sets.
